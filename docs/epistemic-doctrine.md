# Epistemic Doctrine

ATHENA applies the same discipline to what goes into an analysis and to what comes out of it.

---

## 1. ATHENA ranks hypotheses; it does not predict behaviour

ATHENA maps and ranks **reception hypotheses**. It does not forecast what an audience will do.

This is not a cosmetic caution. It determines how the framework is validated: concordance is assessed on the **hierarchy of retained hypotheses**, not on whether a behavioural threshold was crossed.

## 2. Evidence contract on inputs

Every assessment carries its evidential status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Factual** | Directly supported by the corpus or by documented observation |
| **Deduced** | Inferred by the analyst or the model, with the reasoning stated |
| **Not assessable** | The available data does not allow an assessment |

No evidence, no assessment. An item that cannot be supported stays *not assessable*; it is never filled with a default value. Reports based on incomplete data say so.

Model-generated proposals are subject to the same contract, plus automated checks against unsupported content.

## 3. Proof regimes on outputs

Not all outputs can be verified in the same way. Each ATHENA output is classed in one of four regimes:

| Regime | What the output asserts | How it is validated |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 · Detection** | A property of the message or of the adversarial device | Inter-coder agreement, account forensics, flow metrics. Does not depend on any effect. |
| **R2 · Directly falsifiable prediction** | An effect that leaves a public trace if it occurs | Reception observables: counter-discourse, cascade structure, decay curves, source-indexed rejection. |
| **R3 · Comparative prediction** | An effect whose absence is ambiguous in a single case | Correct ordering across a series of cases, differential observables, baselines. |
| **R4 · Inference on internal states** | A belief or perception of the audience | Audit of the reasoning; empirical validation only through experimental designs. Stated as a hypothesis, never as a finding. |

Rule: **no output without a declared regime**. R2 and R3 outputs carry their expected observables; R4 outputs are worded as hypotheses.

## 4. Observability follows the poles

Classifying the outputs reveals a regularity:

- **CORPS** produces mostly detections (R1): properties of the message.
- **BOUCLIER** produces mostly falsifiable predictions (R2): reactance is noisy, a braced audience leaves public traces.
- **VECTEUR** produces R2 and R3: behaviour is observable, its absence is ambiguous.
- **TERRAIN** produces R3 and R4: adhesion is silent.

In other words, ATHENA is most verifiable precisely where it is most counter-intuitive (reactance, source derogation, rebound), and least verifiable where most approaches claim to measure (adhesion).

## 5. Engagement is not belief

Many people adhere without sharing, and share without believing. Likes and shares are poor measures of belief. Validation never treats engagement metrics as evidence of adhesion.

## 6. Declared veracity

ATHENA diagnoses reception. It does not certify facts. The veracity status of a content is **declared by the analyst**, never produced by the framework. This keeps ATHENA from drifting into a technocratic arbiter of truth.

## 7. Calibration

ATHENA is being calibrated on a series of retrospective cases. Until that series is complete, new analytical axes enter as observation registers rather than scored criteria, so that results remain comparable across versions.
