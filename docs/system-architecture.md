# System Architecture

## Overview

ATHENA is organised in three successive stages:

1. **Upstream material**: layers that describe the narrative, the attacker's methods and the state of the terrain. They produce material, not scores.
2. **Objective setting**: an inferential step that fixes what the narrative is trying to achieve.
3. **Pole assessment**: the four poles assess the reception hypothesis, knowing the objective.

The interaction of the poles produces a **configuration**. That configuration, not any single score, is the main object of ATHENA analysis.

---

## Stage 1 — Upstream Material

### Archetypal reading

The archetypal reading answers three questions: *who speaks, which role does it assign to others, and where is it sliding?*

- The emitter's **figure** is identified within a canonical set of archetypes, together with its **state** (light or shadow). Light and shadow are states of one figure, not separate archetypes.
- The **roles assigned** to the target, the adversary or third parties are read. A common trap is the devaluing role imposed on the other (the emitter as guardian of order, the other as disruptor).
- The **slide** is qualified when the corpus offers two moments to compare. The arrival state determines the valence: a slide towards shadow is destructive, towards light constructive.
- The **implicit target** is inferred: the message is a key cut for a lock. This is always a hypothesis. When the implicit target diverges from the audience actually reached, the gap is itself a reactance signal.

The archetypal reading is identified once and shared with the poles as a descriptive fact. Sharing a fact does not break the poles' blindness to each other; sharing a score would.

### Attacker-side frameworks

ATHENA ingests attacker-side descriptions as upstream material:

- **[DISARM](https://github.com/disarmfoundation)**: tactics, techniques, phases of the campaign
- **[DIMA](https://github.com/M82-project/DIMA)** (M82 project): cognitive techniques used by the manipulation

These describe *how* the attack is built. They inform objective setting and specific assessments, but they remain on the attacker's plane. They are never aggregated into the audience-side poles: doing so would score the attacker where ATHENA scores the target.

### Reception substrate

The reception substrate describes the terrain **before** the message arrives: institutional distrust, polarization as an ambient condition, accumulated adversity, social demand for certain narratives.

Without it, an analysis remains centred on the supply of narratives and misses why some audiences actively look for them.

---

## Stage 2 — Objective Setting

Before any assessment, two objectives are fixed:

- the **immediate narrative objective**: what this device aims at here and now, inferred primarily from the archetypal reading, with attacker-side techniques as constraining evidence;
- the **long-term strategic aim**: the durable purpose (eroding a trust, altering a group's self-narrative), defined against the reception substrate.

"The narrative works" covers three different output variables:

| Variable | Question |
|---|---|
| Perception | Does it alter how something is seen? |
| Belief | Does it install or reinforce a belief? |
| Behaviour | Does it trigger the intended action? |

They are not measured the same way. The relevant one is fixed at this stage, so that the poles assess *whether the narrative reaches its identified objective*, not whether it "works" in the abstract.

Objective setting precedes assessment by design: the poles must not define the target they assess.

---

## Stage 3 — The Four Poles

### CORPS — narrative structure

Causal chain, clarity, coherence, emotional congruence, mastery of archetypal roles, framing logic.

*What kind of narrative object is being presented?*

### TERRAIN — audience configuration

Value compatibility, identity proximity to the emitter, moral compatibility, cognitive availability, dominant cognitive vulnerability, informational saturation, and the dynamics of the narrative field (tension, resilience, half-life).

*What kind of environment is receiving the message?*

### BOUCLIER — reactance and defensive threshold

BOUCLIER is derived from a dedicated **reactance module**. It distinguishes:

- reactance **carried by the message**: what in its form, tone or source can be received as a restriction of the freedom to judge;
- reactance **carried by the context**: what in the audience's history and social environment predisposes it to resist, whatever the message.

In line with reactance theory, the expected effects are derogation of the source and reinforcement of the threatened position.

A high score means a high risk of **rejection** by the audience, not dangerousness of the content.

BOUCLIER remains the core differentiator of ATHENA: reactance is treated as a structural pole, not as a side effect.

### VECTEUR — activation and propagation

What the narrative asks of its audience, how feasible that action is, what the diffusion ecosystem allows, and whether the moment is right.

*Can the audience, concretely and at the right moment, act and pass it on?*

---

## Cross-Cutting Elements

### Temporal layer

- **Narrative half-life**: how long a dominant narrative holds before being displaced in the audience's ecosystem.
- **Narrative phase**: incubation, trigger, amplification, sedimentation, crystallisation.
- **Case mode**: one-shot or cumulative. Scoring a single message from a saturation campaign would assess the wrong level; the mode is decided before assessment.

### Declared veracity

The analyst declares the veracity status of the content. ATHENA never produces it: the framework diagnoses reception, it does not certify facts. The same BOUCLIER level then reads differently: healthy resistance to a falsehood, or a pathology of rejection against a true message.

### Methodological safeguard

New analytical axes (temporal, strategic, sequence between techniques) enter first as **observation registers**, described but not scored, until back-testing shows they carry signal. Each new scored axis deepens the map but weakens validation; ATHENA sequences them rather than accumulating them.

---

## Outputs

- **Configuration diagnosis**: balance of forces, dominant pole, vigilance level, recommended posture.
- **Signals**: named configurations triggered by specific combinations of factors (for instance a risk of reactance against the emitter, or a source whose credibility poisons the message).
- **Analyst report**: full reasoning, evidential status of each element, points left to arbitration.
- **Decision-maker briefing**: jargon-free, distinguishing what is verified from what is inferred.

---

## Example Configurations

| Configuration | Reading |
|---|---|
| CORPS strong + TERRAIN favourable + BOUCLIER high | Failure or rejection |
| CORPS average + TERRAIN aligned + BOUCLIER low + VECTEUR strong | Amplification |
| VECTEUR strong + BOUCLIER high | Reactance / polarization |
| CORPS weak + TERRAIN unstable | Weak attack on fragile terrain: the risk is the defenders' over-reaction |

## Dynamic Reading

ATHENA supports a dynamic reading of narrative situations: stability zones, fragility zones, tension zones and possible tipping points. Three kinds of tipping point are identified as observation markers:

- **Threshold**: the narrative overflows its initial niche through a relay.
- **Function switch**: the narrative stops persuading and starts coordinating a group.
- **Proof event**: a real event retroactively validates a dormant narrative and collapses resistance.
